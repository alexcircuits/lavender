## How to read it

- **One row per feature.** IDs (`F-001`…) are stable. Tasks, tests and the migration plan refer to them. The Dependencies column names other F-ids that must land first or alongside.
- **Paths** are relative to `products/lavender/`. `legacy/...` is GEV as vendored. `:n` line references come from the audits.
- **Notation:**
  - `feed:<id>` means a `GET /api/v1/feeds/<id>` snapshot plus a stream subscription (ADR 0005).
  - "core" is `lavender-core`, "catalog" is `GET /api/v1/catalog`, and "status" is `GET /api/v1/status`.
  - "Custom protocol" is the desktop app's `lavender://` URI-scheme handler. It serves tile and binary resources from the core, because the desktop build has no HTTP server (ADR 0002).
- **Tests** names the legacy suites whose cases are ported (Rust for backend behaviour, Vitest for pure TS), plus the new checks that prove the row. Playwright means end-to-end.
- **Cells are fragments.** The authoritative detail stays in the cited audit section.

## Categories

| Category | Meaning | Phase rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Preserve** | Port the behaviour largely as it is. The logic may be re-expressed in TS or Rust, but users see the same capability and semantics | P1–P7 |
| **Improve** | Keep the capability; rework its design, performance, provenance or structure | P1–P7 |
| **Replace** | Keep the underlying need; build it differently (a new mechanism, provider or surface) | P1–P7 |
| **Remove** | Drop it. Legacy share-link tokens that refer to it decode to a visible notice, never an error | — |
| **Defer** | Not in the first delivery. Built in P5 (Automation & deferred), or left to the Roadmap pending a product or policy decision | P5 or Roadmap |

## Significant decisions explained

**Military aircraft merged into one aircraft engine (F-017–F-019).** GEV's military layer is a fork of flights: about 2,800 duplicated lines whose units have drifted apart, plus a registry that polls `/v2/mil` every 60 s even when the layer is off. Lavender keeps the behaviour (a distinct military view and style) but sets an `Aircraft.military` flag during the core merge. One renderer, one motion model and one track API serve both. The layer becomes a filter, and old `m` share tokens map to "aircraft, military filter".

**adsb.lol is the default; OpenSky is evaluation-only (F-020, F-021).** OpenSky's terms forbid for-profit and operational live-product use without a written licence. adsb.lol is ODbL, which a product can use with attribution. OpenSky is still implemented with its OAuth and credit governor, but it is registered as `RequiresAgreement`. Commercial mode therefore disables it unless the operator records an agreement (spec §8 Q2).

**Fake sensor modes removed; two redesigned as honest imagery adjustments (F-117–F-128).** None of CRT, NVG, FLIR, Anime, Noir or Snow uses sensor data. FLIR prints a fabricated temperature, NVG prints a fake clock, and the IR boost invents heat signatures. Several of them distort geometry so clicks hit the wrong object, and they hold continuous rendering. Only two intents have analytic value: luminance false colour and a monochrome basemap. Those return as one static, labelled stage ("derived from RGB brightness — not thermal"). Everything else is removed. The same reasoning removes the scope mask, label fade, cyber theme and sonar, bloom, and the HUD's classification banners and random ids.

**HUD replaced by a status bar; cockpit replaced by a generic Follow/Viewpoint camera (F-134, F-098).** The HUD's useful fields (UTC, MSL altitude, footprint, provenance) move to a status bar that reports the cursor and view centre rather than the camera sub-point. The cockpit's analytic core (smooth following, ground clamp, proximity roster, stale and lost honesty) becomes a camera mode for any moving track, with free look and labels such as "heading derived from course; no attitude data". Its visor, fake attitude indicator, clouds and TR-3B are removed.

**Labels engine moved to P5; detection decoration removed (F-132, F-133).** The "detection" overlay is the largest steady-state frame cost and detects nothing. Its declutter and density maths and its accessible list are valuable, so they return as a "Labels" service in P5. Until then, P2–P4 label only selected and tracked entities. This keeps the vertical slices within the performance budget.

**Voice deferred to P5, API-key path only (F-155–F-158).** Voice only works well on top of a single typed command registry (F-150). GEV had two overlapping registries, 56 tools and a 5,000-line runner. Voice also needs server-side cost enforcement, explicit consent for microphone and imagery, and redaction of Google and CCTV content. P5 builds the registry first (palette, keyboard and MCP use it), then voice as a thin adapter.

**ChatGPT/Codex OAuth dropped (F-160).** The path reads another tool's credential file and spawns `codex login`. OpenAI does not sanction it for third-party products, and upstream itself warns that it may breach terms. Only NoirSystems' or the operator's own API key is supported.

**Submarine cables licence-gated, not deleted (F-048).** The TeleGeography snapshot has no commercial right, and even its non-commercial basis is unverified. The owner's policy forbids deleting restricted assets. The layer is therefore built with a registry entry that commercial mode refuses ("disabled by licence policy"), a build flag that keeps the files out of commercial bundles, and a recorded OSM alternative (licensing Q1).

**CCTV off by default, Street View fallback removed, projection deferred (F-056–F-060).** Camera frames can show identifiable people, and several packs have unknown or restrictive terms. CCTV is `privacy: Sensitive`, so it is off in every mode, carries an in-product notice and has per-jurisdiction switches (unknown-terms packs start off). The Google Street View fallback is removed: it spent quota from an ungated route, accepted a client-chosen location, and breached Google's "same screen" rule. The ground-heights file was sampled from Google 3D Tiles, so it is regenerated from open terrain. The 3D frame projection and viewshed wait for the policy review.

**ALPR deferred to the Roadmap (F-061).** The layer maps surveillance infrastructure, leaks the viewport to a community host, and needs per-market legal review (licensing §7, Q21). The spec's scope table (§4.1) does not include it. It is recorded, not built.

**Key-setup HTTP endpoint dropped on the web server (F-176).** A server that writes secrets over HTTP and restarts itself is the wrong model for a hosted product (ADR 0006). On the web, operators set keys through environment variables or secret files and restart. On the desktop, each key goes to the OS keychain through a narrowly-scoped Tauri command that never returns the value. GEV's admission-gate rules stay documented as the reference for any future administrative endpoint.

**Browser keys delivered at runtime; Google 3D through a token route (F-006, F-178).** GEV compiled three keys into its bundle, so changing one meant restarting the server. Lavender bakes in no keys. Operator-marked public keys arrive through the catalog, and Google 3D Tiles can use a short-lived token minted by the core. That token also solves referrer-restricted keys failing from the `tauri://` origin.

**NASA GIBS replaces Esri as the default basemap; Bing removed (F-003, F-008, F-009).** The keyless Esri endpoint has no commercial grant. The Bing stacks are retiring and may not be combined with other imagery. GIBS (US Government) plus Cesium's bundled Natural Earth II gives a commercially safe online and offline default. Esri, OSM, Google and ion remain available as keyed or configured stacks.

**Raw Overpass QL is no longer a client API (rust-migration #37; F-049, F-105).** GEV accepted arbitrary QL from the browser behind a regex sanitizer. Lavender builds every query in the core from typed templates (installations, building outlines, map features). The sanitizer is still ported with its full test corpus, as defence in depth for operator-configured and MCP-originated queries.

**Workspace persistence is local in P3; server-stored workspaces are Roadmap (F-110).** Spec §4.3 asks for a workspace document persisted through the core, while overview §7 and ADR 0006 defer multi-user workspace storage. Both hold: P3 ships the versioned, validated document, persisted per device (SQLite on desktop, the preference store on web) and shareable through deep links. Server-side ids and short links need authentication and arrive with the roadmap's collaboration work.

**Radio deferred, FM and SDR split (F-065–F-067).** The two audits disagree: the layers audit keeps radio for a later phase, while the UI audit removes it as entertainment. The phase plan places radio in P5, so the station directory and playback are deferred (with disclosed IP exposure) rather than removed. Browser FM demodulation is removed. The WebUSB ADS-B decoder goes to the Roadmap, because WebUSB does not exist in WKWebView or WebKitGTK; first-party ADS-B instead arrives as Rust ingest of decoder feeds (F-026, P5).

**Bhote Koshi removed; event packs deferred (F-053–F-055).** The Nepal pack is CC BY-NC data wired into ~7,300 lines of bespoke code that injects social-media SDKs. It is removed from core. Its reusable idea, a provenance-carrying event pack (licence, capture time, verification status, sha256), becomes a generic format in P5, of which the Nepal pack may become an evaluation-only example.

**Launches split from the space-missions visualisation (F-032, F-033).** The launch list and pad markers are cheap and useful once payload matching moves to the core, which removes GEV's multi-megabyte TLE download into the browser. The reconstructed trajectories, replay and chase camera are presentation-heavy and weakly tested, so they wait for P5.

**Military awareness becomes an inspector section (F-050).** "Contacts" was a layer that reached into other layers' internals and forced other modes. Its value, a proximity roster around a subject, becomes a core spatial query that serves any selected entity. It keeps the explicit line "n inputs unknown · not an all-clear".
